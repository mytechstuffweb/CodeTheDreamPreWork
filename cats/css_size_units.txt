Relative Units:
================
Relative units are dynamic and adjust based on factors 
like the parent element's size, the root element's font size, 
or the viewport dimensions. They are crucial for creating responsive 
web designs that adapt to different screen sizes. 

- em: Relative to the computed font-size of the parent element. 
    This unit can be useful for maintaining consistent scaling within a component.
- rem: Relative to the font-size of the root <html> element. 
    This provides a more predictable and consistent scaling mechanism 
    across the entire document.
- % (Percentage): Represents a percentage of the parent element's corresponding 
    dimension (e.g., width: 50% means 50% of the parent's width).
- vw (Viewport Width): 1% of the viewport's width.
- vh (Viewport Height): 1% of the viewport's height.
- vmin (Viewport Minimum): 1% of the smaller dimension (width or height) 
    of the viewport.
- vmax (Viewport Maximum): 1% of the larger dimension (width or height) 
    of the viewport.
- ch: Represents the width of the "0" (zero) character in the element's current font. 
    Useful for controlling line length in typography.
- ex: Represents the x-height of the element's current font, 
    typically the height of lowercase "x."

Choosing the Right Unit:
=========================
The choice of CSS unit depends on the specific design requirements 
and the need for responsiveness. Relative units like rem, em, %, vw, and vh 
are generally preferred for creating flexible and accessible layouts 
that adapt well to various devices and user preferences. 
Absolute units like px are suitable when a fixed, 
pixel-perfect measurement is necessary.
